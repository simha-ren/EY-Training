# Soil Health and Irrigation Guidance (Advisory)

## Soil testing
A soil test reports pH, organic carbon, and major nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium). Applying fertilizer based on a soil-test recommendation avoids both under- and over-application, saving cost and protecting the soil. Test before each main season where possible.

## Balanced nutrition
Over-reliance on a single nutrient (often nitrogen) degrades soil structure and can worsen pest pressure. Balanced nutrient management combines organic matter (compost, green manure) with recommended doses of major and micro nutrients based on the soil test.

## Efficient irrigation
Drip and sprinkler systems deliver water near the root zone and cut losses compared with flood irrigation, which matters most in water-scarce regions. Scheduling irrigation by crop stage and soil moisture, rather than fixed intervals, improves water-use efficiency.

## Seasonal and local variation
Crop choice, water availability, and recommended practices depend heavily on local rainfall, soil type, and season (kharif vs rabi). Treat these as general principles and confirm specifics with local extension services.
