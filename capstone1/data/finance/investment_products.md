# Comparing Common Investment Products (Educational)

## Fixed deposits vs mutual funds
A fixed deposit (FD) offers a guaranteed, fixed rate of return and capital protection, making it low risk but with returns that may not beat inflation. Mutual funds pool money across investors and invest in equity or debt; they carry market risk but historically offer higher long-term returns. FDs suit short horizons and capital preservation; equity mutual funds suit long horizons and growth.

## SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)
An SIP invests a fixed amount at regular intervals into a mutual fund. It enforces discipline and averages the purchase price across market cycles (rupee-cost averaging), reducing the risk of mistiming a lump-sum entry.

## Risk and diversification
A core principle is diversification: spreading money across asset classes (equity, debt, gold, cash) so a fall in one is cushioned by others. Higher expected return generally comes with higher volatility. Your asset mix should reflect your time horizon and risk tolerance, not market noise.

## Tax-advantaged options
Instruments such as ELSS mutual funds and the Public Provident Fund (PPF) offer tax deductions under prevailing tax law, but carry lock-in periods (3 years for ELSS, 15 years for PPF). Lock-in trades liquidity for the tax benefit.
